My PhD investigated the foundational theory of ontological relations — the building blocks that structure how entities in an ontology relate to one another. Relations such as part-of, depends-on, constitutes, and causes are used pervasively in applied ontologies, but their formal definitions are often inconsistent, ambiguous, or left implicit. FORT proposes a unified, logically grounded framework for defining, comparing, and composing these relations within OWL 2 DL.

The work combines formal philosophy (mereology, ontological dependence) with description logic and semantic web engineering. A key contribution is a modular ontology design pattern library — reusable, well-documented axiom sets for each relation type — that can be imported and adapted by domain ontologists without revisiting foundational choices from scratch.

Thesis outline

  • I
    Introduction & Problem Statement
    The need for formal, reusable definitions of ontological relations. Motivating examples from biomedical, industrial, and environmental ontologies.
  • II
    Background: Ontological Relations in the Literature
    Survey of existing accounts of mereology, dependence, constitution, causation, and participation in philosophy and formal ontology.
  • III
    FORT: A Foundational Relations Theory
    The core theoretical contribution — a taxonomy of relation types with precise, OWL-expressible axiomatisations and cross-relation composition rules.
  • IV
    Implementation as Modular Design Patterns
    Packaging FORT as reusable OWL 2 DL modules. Validation with SHACL, tooling support, and integration guidelines for domain ontologists.
  • V
    Applications & Evaluation
    Case studies applying FORT to [domain ontologies used — fill in]. Evaluation of expressivity, consistency, and ontological commitments.
  • VI
    Conclusion & Perspectives
    Contributions, limitations, and the path from foundational theory to applied ontology engineering in sustainability and circular economy contexts.

Key contributions

  • Relation taxonomy
    A principled classification of ontological relation types with formal definitions in OWL 2 DL and first-order logic.
  • FORT design patterns
    A library of modular, reusable OWL 2 DL axiom sets for foundational relations, ready to import into domain ontologies.
  • Composition rules
    Formal characterisation of which relations compose with which, and what the result relation type is — preventing common modelling errors.

    Demonstration that FORT patterns improve consistency and reduce alignment effort in [domains — fill in].
